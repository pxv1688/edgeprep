15Three doors, one prize. You pick a door, the host opens a different empty door and offers a switch. What is your probability of winning if you switch?

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 2/3

Switching wins whenever the first pick was wrong, which happens with probability 2/3.

  1. Your first pick is right with probability ⅓ and wrong with probability ⅔.
  2. The host always opens an empty door you didn't pick, which gives no new information about your own door.
  3. If your first pick was wrong (⅔), the prize is behind the one remaining closed door, so switching wins.
  4. P(win by switching) = 2/3.

22You roll a fair 4-sided die 4 times. What is the probability that a 4 appears at least once? (Decimal is fine.)

ComplementsWarm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 175/256 ≈ 0.6836

It's easier to find the chance it never appears. Each roll misses with probability 3/4, and the rolls are independent, so P(never) = (3/4)^4 ≈ 0.3164. So P(at least once) = 1 − 0.3164 ≈ 0.6836.

23You roll two fair 4-sided dice. What is the probability the sum is exactly 5?

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 1/4

There are 4 × 4 = 16 equally likely ordered outcomes. The ones summing to 5 are (1,4), (2,3), (3,2), (4,1): 4 in total. So the probability is 4/16 = 1/4.

28You have $2 and bet $1 on fair coin flips until you reach $5 or go broke. What is the probability you reach $5?

Answer: 2/5

In a fair game your expected wealth stays at $2. You finish with $5 (probability p) or $0, so 5p = 2 and p = 2/5.

29You draw 2 independent Uniform(0,1) numbers. What is the expected value of the largest?

Order statisticsInterview

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 2/3

The max is below t only if all 2 draws are, so P(max ≤ t) = t^2 and its density is 2t^1. Then E[max] = ∫₀¹ t × 2t^1 dt = 2/3. (General rule: n/(n+1).)

30You draw 2 independent Uniform(0,1) numbers. What is the expected value of the smallest?

Order statisticsInterview

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 1/3

Flip every draw to 1 − X: the smallest becomes the largest. The largest of 2 uniforms averages 2/3, so the smallest averages 1 − 2/3 = 1/3.

31A fair die has 2 faces. What is the expected number of rolls to see every face at least once? (Decimal.)

Coupon collectorInterview

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 3

Split the wait into stages. After seeing i faces, a new face appears with probability (2 − i)/2, so that stage takes 2/(2 − i) rolls on average. Add the stages: 2/2 + 2/1 + … + 2/1 = 2 × (1 + 1/2 + … + 1/2) ≈ 3.

32Each $1 bet wins with probability 0.4. Starting with $3, you play until you have $6 or nothing. What is the probability you reach $6? (Decimal.)

Gambler's ruinHard

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 8/35 ≈ 0.2286

r = 1.5, so ((1.5)^3 − 1)/((1.5)^6 − 1) ≈ 0.2286.

  1. Let Pₖ be the chance of reaching $6 from $k. One bet later: Pₖ = 0.4·Pₖ₊₁ + 0.6·Pₖ₋₁, with P₀ = 0 and P6 = 1.
  2. Try Pₖ = A + B·rᵏ. Substituting shows it works when r = q/p = 0.6/0.4 = 1.5.
  3. The boundary conditions fix A and B: Pₖ = (rᵏ − 1)/(r^6 − 1).
  4. At k = 3: (1.5^3 − 1)/(1.5^6 − 1) = 2.375 / 10.39 ≈ 0.2286.
