Options interview questions

Trading interviews expect fluency with option payoffs, put–call parity, the Greeks and quick pricing approximations. These questions cover the ideas that come up most, from delta hedging to Black–Scholes.

1Roughly what is the delta of an at-the-money call with a short time to expiry?

  1. 0.25
  2. 0.5
  3. 0.75
  4. 1.0

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 0.5

Near the money the option is close to a coin flip, so delta ≈ 0.5.

2Which is put–call parity for European options on a non-dividend stock?

  1. C + P = S + K
  2. C − P = S − Ke^(−rT)
  3. C − P = K − S
  4. C = P

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: C − P = S − Ke^(−rT)

Long call plus short put pays S_T − K at expiry, the same as a forward.

3Buying a straddle (call + put, same strike) is mainly a bet that…

  1. the price rises
  2. the price falls
  3. the price moves a lot either way
  4. rates rise

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: the price moves a lot either way

A straddle profits from large moves, so it is long volatility.

4The theta of a long vanilla option is usually…

  1. positive
  2. negative
  3. zero
  4. equal to gamma

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: negative

Time decay erodes value, so theta is negative for the holder.

5A deep in-the-money call has a delta close to…

  1. 0
  2. 0.5
  3. 1
  4. −1

Warm-up

It will almost surely be exercised, so it moves one-for-one with the stock.

6A put's delta lies in which range?

  1. 0 to 1
  2. −1 to 0
  3. −0.5 to 0.5
  4. 1 to 2

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: −1 to 0

Put delta = call delta − 1, so it lies between −1 and 0.

7The gamma of a long option position (call or put) is…

8A call spread (long a K₁ call, short a K₂ call, K₁ < K₂) has a maximum payoff of…

13What is the price of a European call with strike 0 on a stock that pays no dividends?

  1. 0
  2. The stock price
  3. The strike
  4. It can't be priced

Warm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: The stock price

With K = 0 you always exercise and receive the stock, so the call is worth S.

14Zero rates, no dividends. Stock $90, a $90 call costs $6.34. What should the $90 put with the same expiry cost?

Put–call parityWarm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 317/50 ≈ 6.34

A long call plus a short put pays S − K at expiry no matter what, the same as owning the stock and owing K. With zero rates their prices must match: C − P = S − K. So P = C − S + K = 6.34 − 90 + 90 = $6.34.

16You buy a $50 straddle for a total premium of $3.5. What is the upper breakeven stock price at expiry?

StrategiesWarm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 107/2

A straddle pays |S − K| at expiry. You only profit once the move from $50 covers the $3.5 you paid, so the upper breakeven is 50 + 3.5 = 53.5.

17You buy a $50 straddle for a total premium of $3.5. What is the lower breakeven stock price at expiry?

StrategiesWarm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 93/2

A straddle pays |S − K| at expiry. On the downside the stock must fall far enough below $50 to cover the $3.5 premium: 50 − 3.5 = 46.5.

18You are long 1 call contract (100 shares each), delta 0.4. How many shares must you sell to be delta-neutral?

Delta hedgingWarm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 40

Each contract covers 100 shares, so your delta is 1 × 100 × 0.4 = 40 shares' worth of exposure. Selling that many shares cancels it.

19You are long 1 put contract (100 shares each), delta −0.4. How many shares must you buy to be delta-neutral?

Delta hedgingWarm-up

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 40

Puts gain when the stock falls, so their delta is negative: 1 × 100 × (−0.4) = −40 shares. Buying 40 shares brings the total to zero.

22If implied volatility doubles, an at-the-money option's price roughly…

  1. stays the same
  2. doubles
  3. quadruples
  4. halves

23Vega is largest for which option?

29A delta-hedged position has gamma 0.02 per share. The stock moves $1. Approximately how much does the hedged position gain, per share?

GammaInterview

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 1/100 ≈ 0.01

Delta hedging cancels the first-order (straight-line) P&L. What's left is the curvature term from a Taylor expansion: ½ × Γ × (ΔS)² = ½ × 0.02 × 1² = 0.01. Long gamma gains whichever way the stock moves.

31Black–Scholes call price: spot $100, strike $80, vol 15%, 1 year to expiry, rate 5%. (To the cent.)

Black–ScholesHard

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 24.08

Call = S N(d₁) − K e^(−rT) N(d₂) ≈ $24.08.

  1. Inputs: S = 100, K = 80, σ = 0.15, T = 1, r = 0.05.
  2. d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T) = [0.2231 + 0.06125] / 0.15 ≈ 1.896.
  3. d₂ = d₁ − σ√T ≈ 1.746. The discount factor is e^(−rT) ≈ 0.9512.
  4. Call = S N(d₁) − K e^(−rT) N(d₂) = 100 × 0.971 − 80 × 0.9512 × 0.9596 ≈ $24.08.

32Black–Scholes call price: spot $100, strike $80, vol 30%, 1 year to expiry, rate 0%. (To the cent.)

Black–ScholesHard

Show the answer and solution

Answer: 23.53

Call = S N(d₁) − K e^(−rT) N(d₂) ≈ $23.53.

  1. Inputs: S = 100, K = 80, σ = 0.3, T = 1, r = 0.
  2. d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T) = [0.2231 + 0.045] / 0.3 ≈ 0.8938.
  3. d₂ = d₁ − σ√T ≈ 0.5938. The discount factor is e^(−rT) ≈ 1.
  4. Call = S N(d₁) − K e^(−rT) N(d₂) = 100 × 0.8143 − 80 × 1 × 0.7237 ≈ $23.53.
